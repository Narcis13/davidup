## Limitations that cost the most authoring effort

| # | Limitation | What it forced in the showcase |
|---|---|---|
| **L-1** | A composition can't reference global-library **templates or behaviors** by id; `precompile` sees only `templates{}`, built-ins and the process registry. There is no composition-scoped `behaviors{}` block, so an executable user behavior only works inside an MCP/editor session. | `build.mjs` read `~/.davidup/library/templates/ctaButton.template.json` and inlined it; `render.ts` called `registerBehavior()` before compiling. **Fixed in G4** — the showcase names `global:ctaButton` and carries `neonFlicker` in its own `behaviors{}` block, and `davidup render` renders the film. |
| **L-2** | A root **scene instance can't be owned by a group** (`E_INVALID_VALUE: Scene instance "orr0" has no layerId and is not referenced by any layer`). | **L-4** | The expression language has only `+ − * / %`, `min`, `max`, `round`. | **L-5** | No gradient fills (linear/radial) on shapes or text. | The ambient background is three 1000 px circles under a 140 px blur in an isolated group, the most expensive thing in every frame (see P-1). |

## Performance

| # | Finding |
|---|---|
| **P-1** | Measured on an M-series Mac: the final 900 frames took **617 s (1.46 fps average)**, and each 180-frame Droste pass (the reveal: a full-frame video in an isolated group with a `shadow` effect, plus the always-on 140 px aurora blur) took about 120 s (1.5 fps). So speed is low throughout the film, not only in the reveal, which points at the always-on blurred aurora (not measured in isolation yet; profile first). Each isolated/effected group allocates a full-canvas offscreen and blurs it at full resolution every frame. Ideas: (a) size offscreens to the group's transformed bounds, not the canvas; (b) blur large σ on a downscaled surface (σ ≥ 20 → ½ or ¼ res, then upscale; visually identical); (c) cache the offscreen when no descendant has an active tween at `t` (static groups). Numbers are in `examples/showcase-vertical/output/render.log`. **Measured in G8** with the new `davidup render --profile`: 1.58 fps, and **99.1 % of paint time is inside `blurPixels`** — the guess was right, and offscreen *allocation* is nearly free (skia defers it). (b) alone took the film to 5.65 fps; (a) is worth 2.3× on a small blurred item on a big canvas but ~nothing here, and (c) was dropped. |
| **P-2** | `render.ts --stills` compiles the whole composition once per still (≈0.5 s each). The MCP `render_preview_frame` path probably does the same per call. A compile cache keyed on the source hash would make still-scrubbing near-instant. *(unverified for MCP)* |

| **P-3** | With the limiter only (B-9 forced dropping `targetLufs`), the showcase measures **−8.9 LUFS integrated, +0.4 dBFS true peak** after AAC encoding. `alimiter` is sample-peak, not true-peak, so AAC inter-sample overs exceed the ceiling. Consider a true-peak-aware ceiling (oversampled limiter, or lower the default ceiling to −1.5 dBTP when no `targetLufs` is set). |

## Minor / DX

- `renderToFile(comp, "<dir>")` without `format: "png-sequence"` fails with ffmpeg's `Unable to find a suitable output format for '<dir>'`. Detect an existing directory and either switch to PNG output or throw a clear `E_OUTPUT_PATH`.
- `W_TWEEN_TRUNCATED` is emitted once per tween. A `$repeat` that overruns reports N copies. Group by the `$repeat` source when the source map has one.
- Environment, not davidup: Homebrew's `/opt/homebrew/bin/ffprobe` (ffmpeg 8) is broken on this Mac (missing `libx265.215.dylib`), while `ffmpeg` on PATH is 6.0. davidup uses `ffprobe-static`, so renders are unaffected. `brew reinstall ffmpeg` fixes the shell.

### Session G3 — Consume VP9 alpha (B-7)
**Files:** `src/drivers/node/videoExtract.ts:350-374` (`buildExtractArgs`), the `VideoExtractSpec` (needs codec + hasAlpha from the asset), extraction cache hash (bump `VIDEO_EXTRACTION_VERSION`).
**Steps:** when the asset's `codec` is `vp9` (or `vp8`) and `hasAlpha`, emit `-c:v libvpx-vp9` / `libvpx` before `-i`. Always add `-pix_fmt rgba` for PNG output. If `hasAlpha` is unknown (asset not probed), probe `alpha_mode` tag. Test: render a transparent comp to `.webm`, use it as a video item over a solid bg, pixel-probe a transparent corner = the bg colour (mirror the existing ProRes test).
**Verify:** baseline + the new round-trip test.
**Commit:** `fix: VP9 alpha survives frame extraction (B-7)`
**Decided:** `-pix_fmt rgba` is applied *only* when the source carries alpha, not always — forcing RGBA on opaque
sources moves swscale's rounding by up to 3/255 and shifted the three `video-*` goldens for no benefit. The decoder
override is the load-bearing half (verified by disabling each half against the new round-trip test); the explicit
`-pix_fmt` on alpha specs only states what ffmpeg already negotiates from yuva frames. `VideoExtractSpec` gained
`alpha` + `decoder`, both hashed, and `VIDEO_EXTRACTION_VERSION` is 3. `hasAlpha`/`codec` come from the asset;
a `.webm`/`.mkv` asset missing them is probed by a new best-effort `probeVideoSync` (memoised on path+mtime+size so
the three callers that recompute a spec hash agree). Other containers are never probed — their alpha is in the pixel
format, which any decoder honours.

### Session G4 — Library templates/behaviors from a composition (L-1)
**Files:** `src/compose/precompile.ts`, `src/compose/templates.ts`, `src/compose/behaviors.ts`, `src/schema/zod.ts` (top-level `behaviors`), dual schema, README.
**Steps:** (1) composition-scoped `behaviors: { name: descriptor }` registered for the duration of one compile (not into the global overlay), so a comp that defines and uses an executable behavior renders from the CLI. (2) `$template: "global:ctaButton"` / `$behavior: "global:neonFlicker"` resolved from the library root at precompile (Node only; browser gets a clear `E_FEATURE_UNAVAILABLE`). Alternatively make `$ref` accept `global:` paths, which is smaller and composes with (1). Delete the inlining + `registerBehavior` workaround in `examples/showcase-vertical/`.
**Verify:** baseline + `davidup render examples/showcase-vertical/composition.json` works end to end (needs G1).
**Commit:** `feat: composition-scoped behaviors; global: templates/behaviors resolve at compile (L-1)`
**Decided:** both halves, and `$ref` was *not* taught `global:`. (1) `expandBehaviors` reads a top-level
`behaviors{}` and registers it for that one compile (never the process registry), stripping the key like
`templates` / `scenes`; lookup order is composition block → `options.behaviors` (MCP session) → registry →
built-ins. (2) A new pass `resolveLibraryRefs` (`src/compose/libraryRefs.ts`) runs in `precompile` between `$ref`
inlining and the expansion passes: it walks for `global:`-prefixed `$template` / `$behavior`, loads
`<root>/templates/<id>.template.json` / `<root>/behaviors/<id>.behavior.json` transitively, and merges them into
the composition's own compile-time blocks *under the prefixed key* — so no expansion pass learns about the
library and an authored `"global:<id>"` key shadows the file. `$ref` was left alone: it inlines a JSON *value* at
a path, while a library reference is a named definition that also has to work from inside a template body, where
there is no place to hang a `$ref`. Node only (lazy `node:path` + `defaultGlobalLibraryRoot`, so browser bundles
stay clean); a non-Node runtime gets `E_FEATURE_UNAVAILABLE`. No dual-schema change: `behaviors` is a
composition-file block, not an MCP command shape, and `commands.ts` mirrors no authoring block (`templates` /
`scenes` are absent from it too); `CompositionSchema` is likewise unchanged, since it validates the *canonical*
output the pass strips these from. The showcase's `ctaButton` copy and `registerBehavior` call are gone; its
B-8 label patch went with them, so the Follow label sits on the library's point-mode baseline (the 𝕏 mark moved
to clear it) until G5 re-seeds the library.

### Session G7 — Expression functions (L-4)
**Files:** `src/compose/params.ts:321-333, 515-521`, `REPEAT_EXPRESSIONS_DESIGN.md`, `tests/compose/params.test.ts`.
**Steps:** add `sin cos tan atan2 sqrt pow abs floor ceil clamp(x,a,b) lerp(a,b,t)` and the constant `pi`. All are pure IEEE math, so determinism holds, but note that `Math.sin` is not guaranteed bit-identical across JS engines. Round results to 1e-9 before use in positions, or document the node↔browser tolerance. Rewrite the showcase iris with `x: "${cos(...) * r}"` if it reads better.
**Verify:** baseline + tests per function + an error test for arity.
**Commit:** `feat: trig / sqrt / floor / clamp in template expressions`
**Decided:** all of the listed names plus `pi`, in one table
(`FUNCTIONS` in `src/compose/params.ts`) that carries each entry's arity, its
implementation and whether it is *approximated* — so the parser, the arity
check, the `unknown name` hint, the `E_TEMPLATE_EXPR` hint and `$repeat`'s
reserved-`as` list are all generated from one place and can't drift (`as:
"cos"` is now rejected). Determinism: **rounded**, not documented as a
tolerance. The six calls ECMAScript leaves implementation-approximated
(`sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `atan2`, `sqrt`, `pow`) have their results gridded to
1e-9; everything else is IEEE-exact in every engine and is left untouched, so
no existing expression moves. A compiled composition gets written back to disk
and diffed (editor save, MCP `replace_composition`), which is what makes
"same numbers on node and in the browser" worth a grid rather than a note; the
grid also makes `cos(pi / 2)` read as 0. Values above 1e12 skip it, since 1e-9
is finer than the double's own spacing there and `pow(10, 300)` must not round
to Infinity. Non-finite results fail at the call (`sqrt(-1)`, `pow(10, 400)`),
with the caret on the function name rather than on the whole expression.
`clamp(x, lo, hi)` errors when `lo > hi` instead of silently returning `hi`;
`lerp`'s `t` is unclamped on purpose. Unchanged and worth knowing: a `${…}` is
still only parsed as an expression when it mentions `params.` / `$.` / a loop
variable, so `"${cos(pi)}"` standing alone stays literal text — the v0.3
content-string pin. The showcase iris was rewritten (it reads better): each dot
now flies out on plain `transform.x` / `y` tweens to `cos`/`sin` of its angle
instead of carrying a rotation and a tweened large-negative `anchorX`. Dot
centres match the old layout to within 0.008 px (the old angle constant was a
6-decimal literal) — but the dots no longer rotate, so their antialiasing
differs and the act's frames are not byte-identical. No golden covers the
showcase.

### Session G8 — Offscreen and blur cost (P-1)
**Files:** `src/engine/render.ts` (isolate/effects offscreen path), `src/engine/effects*.ts`.
**Steps:** measure first (`examples/showcase-vertical` per-act fps; add a `--profile` flag to `render.ts` or the CLI). Then: offscreens sized to transformed bounds; downsampled blur for σ ≥ 20; optional static-subtree cache. Each must keep goldens byte-identical or be marked ⚠.
**Verify:** baseline + goldens + before/after fps table in the commit message.
**Commit:** `perf: bounds-sized offscreens and downsampled large blurs`
**Decided:** measured first, and the measurement decided the rest. A new
`davidup render --profile[=<band seconds>]` (engine counters on
`RenderOptions.profile`, per-frame records on `RenderToFileResult.profile`,
the table in `src/cli/profileReport.ts`) put the showcase at **1.58 fps with
99.1 % of paint inside `blurPixels`**. Surface *allocation* turned out to cost
nothing measurable — skia defers a `new Canvas` — so the offscreen half of P-1
was never the problem.

**(b) downsampled blur — the whole win, ⚠ pixel-changing at σ ≥ 20.** At or
above `BLUR_DOWNSAMPLE_SIGMA` = 20 the region is box-averaged down by
`floor(σ/10)` capped at 4, blurred with the narrower kernel and bilinearly
scaled back, all on premultiplied floats inside `engine/blur.ts` so node and
the browser still agree exactly. The cap is where the win stops: past 4 the
kernel is already noise next to the two full-resolution resample passes. σ =
140 on 1080 × 1920 falls 345 ms → 76 ms; the film goes **1.58 → 5.65 fps**.
Against a full-resolution reference kernel the result is within 3 units of
8-bit alpha. No golden moved, because every blur radius in the repo's
examples, fixtures and goldens is ≤ 10 — but someone's wider blur will render
slightly differently than it did before, which is what the ⚠ is for.

**(a) bounds-sized offscreens — correct, general, and worth nothing *here*.**
`engine/bounds.ts` computes the canvas-space box an item's subtree can paint
in (stroke widths, polygon miter spikes at Canvas2D's default miterLimit 10,
and each descendant's own effect reach added on, then clamped to the canvas),
and the scratch surface is cut to it and composited back at its corner.
Byte-identical, and `effects.pixels.test.ts` proves it by rendering each case
both ways — an empty text item beside the subject forces the whole-canvas path,
since a text extent is exactly what the pass refuses to guess. Worth **2.3×**
on a 120 px blurred square on a 1080 × 1920 canvas, and ~0 on the showcase:
its one expensive blur is the aurora, whose three 1000 px blobs plus a 393 px
kernel reach already cover the frame. Kept anyway — it is the general fix, and
the film is not the only composition.

**Decided: only blur takes the cut-down surface.** Shadow and glow are the
host's Canvas2D shadow state, and skia rasterizes the same 6 px glow one unit
of alpha differently into a 90 × 80 surface than into a 120 × 80 one (measured;
it is why the first cut of this session moved pixels). A scratch surface's
size must not reach the output, so `mustUseWholeCanvas` keeps any stack with a
shadow or a glow — and any group with such a descendant, whose own surface is
cut from its parent's — on the surface it has always had. Blur is where the
time was anyway.

**Decided: no static-subtree cache (c).** The workload it targets does not
exist here: the aurora tweens all three blobs every frame, so the cache would
never hit while costing a deep compare on every frame that it misses.

Also: `anchorWidth`/`anchorHeight` moved to `engine/anchor.ts` (re-exported
from `render.ts`) so the bounds pass can pivot an item the way the renderer
does without a cycle back through the renderer.
